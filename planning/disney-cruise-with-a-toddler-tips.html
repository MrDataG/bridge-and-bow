Disney Cruise with a Toddler: 9 Things We Learned on a 3-Night Sailing

6 MIN READFIRSTHANDBY BRIDGE & BOW

Firsthand — written after our September 2026 Disney Cruise Line sailing with a toddler.

Our daughter turned three on our 3-night Disney Destiny cruise out of Port Everglades. Here are the things that made the trip easier — and the ones we would plan around next time.

1. Get the cruise line's airport transfer if you fly in

We paid $29 per person each way, and it meant we could board with no assigned arrival time. Details in our Port Everglades guide.

2. Pack for seasickness before you need it

We used motion-sickness patches and medication for the adults and the kids as a precaution, and it helped us a lot. Talk to your pediatrician first about anything for a young child.

3. Find the Toy Story splash pad

If your toddler is not potty trained and cannot use the other pools, the splash pad is the pool-day answer. The kiddie pool is for potty-trained children only.

4. Dress for the themed dinner

Your restaurant changes each night: ours were 1923 (night one), World of Marvel (night two), and Pride Lands (night three). Disney Cruise Line sets the rotation and dinner times and only shares them once you are aboard, through the app, so you cannot plan exact outfits before you sail. We dressed to match each one.

5. Use the nursery or kids’ clubs for an adult break

Our daughter is three and under and not potty trained, so she stayed in the It’s a Small World Nursery for an hour while we had a break. The Walt Disney Imagineering Lab, Marvel Super Hero Academy, and Star Wars Cargo Bay kept her busy the rest of the time.

6. Buy the photo pass and plan for character lines

The photo pass we bought covered crew photos onboard and at each stop, including the Castaway Cay beach. Lines vary by character, and one night has many characters spread around so you can fit more photos in.

7. Walk the ship before bedtime

After dinner and before bed, we walked a few floors to see what was available to keep our toddler busy.

8. Consider skipping the Nassau excursion

We had been to Nassau before, so we stayed on board and used the ship’s offerings. If you do go ashore, see our Nassau excursion guide.

9. Three nights is enough for a first cruise

It was enough time to enjoy the ship and two ports, and the kids stayed occupied.
