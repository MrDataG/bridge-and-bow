Toddler Packing List for a Family Cruise

7 MIN READUPDATED AUG 2026BY BRIDGE & BOW

A cruise cabin is small, storage is limited, and laundry service is either expensive or nonexistent depending on your line — so packing for a toddler here is less about bringing everything and more about bringing the right things. This list is organized by what actually matters, not a generic checklist.

Note: This guide reflects general best practices for cruising with a toddler. We'll update it with specifics and real photos after our own Disney Cruise Line sailing in September 2026.

Swim & pool essentials

  • 2–3 swimsuits (one is always wet or drying)
  • Swim diapers — most ship pools and splash areas require them, and they sell out fast in port shops, so don't count on buying there
  • Reef-safe sunscreen — some private islands and ports (Castaway Cay included) restrict standard sunscreen to protect marine life
  • A rash guard or UV swim shirt, since reapplying sunscreen to a moving toddler every 2 hours is its own challenge

Sleep & cabin setup

  • A travel blackout curtain or clip — cabins often have portholes or balcony doors that let in more light than a toddler's used to
  • A lightweight travel sound machine or a phone app downloaded for offline use, since ship WiFi is unreliable
  • Familiar sleep items (specific blanket, stuffed animal) — don't pack these in checked luggage if you're flying to embarkation, keep them in a carry-on

Getting around the ship

  • A stroller that folds small — hallways and elevators are tight, and a full-size stroller becomes a liability fast. An umbrella-style stroller is usually the better call than a home stroller.
  • A baby carrier for excursion days — many shore excursions and tender boats aren't stroller-friendly
  • Cabin door magnets or a small whiteboard to help a toddler recognize "our door" in a long identical hallway

What we'd skip

Bulky pool floats and inflatable toys — most ships restrict large personal flotation devices in their pools, and packing space is better spent elsewhere. A full stock of snacks is usually unnecessary too, since most cruise lines have 24-hour food service included, though a small stash of familiar favorites for picky eaters is still worth the suitcase space.

Our take

Pack for the ship's actual environment — small spaces, constant sun, and unpredictable nap schedules — rather than a generic vacation list. The stroller and swim diaper points above are the two most common first-time-cruiser mistakes we see mentioned in other families' trip reports.
